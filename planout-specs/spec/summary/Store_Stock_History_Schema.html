1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Store Stock History feature provides a structured way to track and audit inventory changes across different store locations. By maintaining a detailed log of every increase or decrease in stock, the system ensures that businesses can accurately monitor product movement, investigate discrepancies, and maintain reliable inventory records.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Define Data Structures: The system establishes clear formats for three core areas: the current stock levels at each store, the history of changes made to that stock, and the store information itself.
2.Establish Response Format: The system defines how inventory history data is presented to users, including features like search, sorting, and pagination to handle large amounts of data efficiently.
3.Execute Data Queries: When a user requests information, the system performs a combined search across stores, current stock, and historical logs to provide a complete picture of inventory activity.
4.Map Data to Records: The system ensures that the information retrieved from the database is accurately translated into the defined formats for current stock, historical logs, and store details.
5.Calculate Totals: As part of the reporting process, the system automatically calculates the total number of items changed (net increase or decrease) to provide quick insights into stock trends.
1.3Key Concepts
–Inventory Audit Log: A permanent record of every stock movement, including the reason for the change and the timestamp, which helps in tracking accountability.
–Data Mapping: The process of ensuring that information stored in the database matches the format required by the application, preventing errors and data inconsistencies.
–Relational Linking: The ability to connect different pieces of information—such as linking a specific product change to a specific store—to provide context to the data.
–Pagination and Filtering: Tools that allow users to navigate through large lists of inventory history by searching for specific dates, product IDs, or store names.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Accuracy: By logging every stock change, the business reduces the risk of "ghost inventory" and ensures that the stock levels shown in the system match reality.
–Enhanced Accountability: With detailed history logs, managers can easily identify when and why stock levels changed, making it easier to spot errors or theft.
–Better Decision Making: The ability to filter and summarize stock history allows leadership to identify trends, such as which products are moving fastest or which stores are experiencing frequent inventory adjustments.
–Operational Efficiency: Standardized data structures ensure that reporting is fast and reliable, saving time for staff who need to reconcile inventory or generate stock reports.
