1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Store Stock History feature provides a comprehensive audit trail of inventory changes across your retail locations. It allows users to track exactly how and when stock levels for specific products have increased or decreased. By connecting individual store data with historical movement records, this feature enables better inventory oversight, reporting, and reconciliation.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Filtering: The system accepts search criteria, including the store name, specific product ID, the type of transaction (e.g., sales or transfers), and a specific date range.
2.Data Retrieval: The system gathers data from three interconnected sources: the history of stock changes, the current stock levels, and the store details.
3.Calculation: The system automatically calculates the net change in quantity (total increases minus total decreases) for the filtered results.
4.Organization: The results are sorted based on your preferences (such as by date or store name) and organized into manageable pages to ensure the interface remains fast and easy to navigate.
5.Display: The final output provides a list of individual stock movements, the total number of records found, and the net quantity change for the selected period.
1.3Key Concepts
–Store Stock History: A chronological log of every inventory adjustment, capturing the "who, what, when, and why" of stock changes.
–Net Quantity Change: A summary calculation that shows the overall impact on inventory (total additions minus total subtractions) for a specific search.
–Pagination: A method of breaking large amounts of data into smaller, readable pages to improve system performance and user experience.
–Filtering: The ability to narrow down large datasets by specific attributes like date, product, or store location to find relevant information quickly.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Accountability: By maintaining a clear history of stock movements, you can easily identify discrepancies and understand the reasons behind inventory fluctuations.
–Enhanced Reporting: The ability to filter by transaction type and date range allows managers to generate accurate reports on sales performance or stock replenishment trends.
–Operational Efficiency: With quick access to historical data, staff can resolve inventory inquiries faster without needing to manually cross-reference multiple paper or digital logs.
–Better Decision Making: Having visibility into the net change of stock helps in planning future inventory needs and identifying stores that may require stock adjustments.
