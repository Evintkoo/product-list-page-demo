1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The Stock History feature provides a detailed audit trail of all inventory changes for a specific product. It allows users to view a chronological list of stock movements, helping them understand when, where, and why stock levels changed, as well as who performed the adjustment.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initial Load: When you open the Stock History page for a product, the system automatically retrieves the full history of stock movements for that item and populates a list of available stores for the filter menu.
2.Filtering: You can narrow down the results by selecting a specific store, entering a transaction type, choosing a date range, or searching by the user who created the record.
3.Search Execution: Once you have set your desired criteria, clicking the "Search" button updates the table to display only the records that match your filters.
4.Reviewing Data: The table displays the history in a clear format, showing the date, store location, type of transaction, the specific quantity change (with clear positive or negative indicators), any associated notes, and the staff member responsible.
1.3Key Concepts
–Stock Movement: Any event that increases or decreases the inventory count for a product (e.g., a new shipment or a customer sale).
–Audit Trail: A record of "who, what, and when" regarding inventory changes, which ensures accountability and helps troubleshoot stock discrepancies.
–Filtered Search: The ability to isolate specific data points—such as viewing only "restock" events at a specific store within a specific week—to make large amounts of data easier to manage.
–Signed Quantity: A display method where increases are shown with a plus sign (e.g., +5) and decreases with a minus sign (e.g., -2), allowing for immediate visual recognition of the stock impact.
1.4Business Value

This feature is essential for maintaining accurate inventory records. By providing a transparent history of stock adjustments, it enables managers to:

* Identify Discrepancies: Quickly spot unauthorized or incorrect stock changes.

* Improve Forecasting: Analyze trends in stock movement types (like sales vs. returns) to better plan future inventory needs.

* Enhance Accountability: Maintain a clear record of staff activity, which is vital for operational oversight and loss prevention.

2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The Stock History feature provides a clear, chronological view of how a specific product’s inventory levels have changed over time. Users can view a detailed log of stock movements—such as additions or subtractions—and use various filters to pinpoint specific transactions, helping them maintain accurate inventory records and audit trail transparency.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initial Page Load: When you open the Stock History page for a product, the system automatically retrieves the full list of available stores to populate the "Store" filter dropdown.
2.Displaying Data: Simultaneously, the system fetches the complete stock history for that product and displays it in a table, showing details like the date, store location, type of movement, quantity changed, and who recorded the entry.
3.Applying Filters: You can narrow down the results by selecting specific criteria, such as a particular store, movement type, date range, or the person who created the entry.
4.Updating Results: Once you click the "Search" button, the system refreshes the table to show only the records that match your selected filters.
2.3Key Concepts
–Stock Movement: A record of any event that changes the quantity of a product, such as a restock, a sale, or an adjustment.
–Filter Criteria: The specific parameters (Store, Type, Date Range, Created By) used to refine the list of history records.
–Audit Trail: The ability to see "who" performed an action and "when" it occurred, which is captured in the "Created By" and "Date" fields.
–Quantity Change: The net impact on stock levels, clearly distinguishing between increases and decreases for each transaction.
2.4Business Value

This feature improves inventory accountability by providing a transparent history of stock adjustments. By allowing users to filter by store, date, or user, the system enables quick troubleshooting of inventory discrepancies, simplifies end-of-period reporting, and ensures that all stock changes are documented and easily searchable for auditing purposes.

3.  Data
3.1Overview

The Stock History feature provides a transparent audit trail for product inventory. It allows users to view a detailed log of every stock movement for a specific product, including when the change occurred, which store was involved, the quantity adjusted, and who performed the action. This feature ensures accountability and helps users track inventory fluctuations over time.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initial Page Load: When a user opens the Stock History page for a specific product, the system automatically retrieves the full history of stock movements for that item.
2.Populating Filters: Simultaneously, the system fetches a list of all available stores to populate the "Store" filter dropdown, allowing users to narrow their search later.
3.Applying Filters: Users can refine the list by selecting specific criteria, such as a particular store, the type of movement, a date range, or the user who created the record.
4.Triggering Search: Once the user clicks the "Search" button, the system applies these filters to the database and updates the results table with the relevant records.
5.Sorting Data: Users can click on table column headers to sort the history (e.g., by date or quantity), which instantly refreshes the view to display the data in the desired order.
3.3Key Concepts
–Stock Movement: Any event that changes the inventory level of a product, categorized by type (e.g., additions or deductions).
–Audit Trail: A chronological record of changes, including the date, the specific store location, and the individual responsible for the entry.
–Dynamic Filtering: The ability to narrow down large volumes of data using specific parameters like date ranges or store locations to find relevant information quickly.
–Sortable Data: The ability to organize the history list by different attributes, such as the most recent changes or the largest quantity adjustments.
3.4Business Value
–Accountability: By tracking who created each stock entry and when, the business can easily identify the source of inventory discrepancies.
–Operational Visibility: Managers gain a clear understanding of how stock levels fluctuate across different store locations, supporting better replenishment and planning decisions.
–Efficient Troubleshooting: The ability to filter and sort historical data allows staff to quickly investigate specific inventory issues without manually searching through paper logs or disconnected records.
