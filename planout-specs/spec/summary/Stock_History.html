1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The Stock History feature provides a detailed audit trail of all inventory changes for a specific product. It allows users to track how, when, and by whom stock levels were adjusted across different store locations. This feature is accessible via the product management section of the application and includes robust filtering and sorting capabilities to help users quickly locate specific transaction records.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Navigation: The user selects "Stock History" from the product tab menu to view the history for a specific item.
2.Data Loading: Upon opening the page, the system automatically retrieves all stock movement records and a list of available stores.
3.Display: The system populates a table showing the history of changes. If no records exist, a "No records found" message is displayed.
4.Filtering: Users can refine the list by selecting specific stores, transaction types, date ranges, or the user who performed the action.
5.Search & Sort: Users can trigger a search or click on table headers (such as Date, Store, or Type) to re-sort the data and find specific information.
6.Review: The table displays key details for each transaction, including the quantity change (shown as positive or negative values), the store involved, and any notes provided.
7.Summary: A total aggregate of all quantity changes based on the current filter settings is displayed for quick reference.
1.3Key Concepts
–Audit Trail: A chronological record of every stock adjustment, identifying who made the change and why.
–Signed Quantity: A display format where stock increases are shown with a plus sign (e.g., +5) and decreases with a minus sign (e.g., -2) for immediate clarity.
–Filtering: The ability to narrow down large lists of data using specific criteria like date ranges, store locations, or staff members.
–Aggregate Summary: A calculated total of all quantity changes currently visible in the filtered list, providing a quick snapshot of net stock movement.
1.4Business Value

This feature enhances inventory transparency and accountability. By maintaining a clear history of stock movements, businesses can:

* Improve Accuracy: Easily identify when and why stock discrepancies occurred.

* Enhance Security: Maintain an audit trail of which staff members are adjusting inventory levels.

* Streamline Operations: Quickly analyze stock trends across different store locations to make better-informed replenishment decisions.

2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The Stock History feature provides a transparent view of how a product’s inventory levels have changed over time. By tracking every movement—such as additions, removals, or adjustments—this feature allows you to see exactly when, where, and why stock levels were modified, helping you maintain accurate inventory records across your different store locations.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initial Load: When you open the product history page, the system automatically retrieves a list of all available stores to populate a selection menu.
2.Data Retrieval: The system simultaneously pulls the complete stock history for the specific product you are viewing.
3.Display: The history is presented in a clear table, showing the date of the change, the store involved, the type of movement, the quantity adjusted, the reason for the change, and who performed the action.
4.Filtering: You can narrow down the list using a variety of filters, including specific stores, movement types, date ranges, or the person who recorded the change.
5.Refinement: Clicking the "Search" button or sorting by table headers updates the view instantly, allowing you to quickly find specific transactions or audit trails.
2.3Key Concepts
–Stock Movement: Any event that changes the quantity of a product, such as a sale, a restock, or a manual adjustment.
–Audit Trail: A chronological record that shows who made a change to the stock and why, ensuring accountability.
–Filtering: The ability to isolate specific data points (like a date range or a specific store) to make large amounts of history easier to analyze.
–Store Mapping: The process of linking inventory data to specific physical or virtual locations, ensuring you know exactly where the stock change occurred.
2.4Business Value
–Improved Accuracy: By maintaining a detailed log of all stock changes, you can easily identify and resolve inventory discrepancies.
–Enhanced Accountability: Knowing exactly who performed a stock adjustment and why provides better oversight of warehouse and store operations.
–Better Decision Making: With clear visibility into historical trends, you can make more informed decisions regarding restocking schedules and inventory management strategies.
–Time Savings: The built-in search and filter tools allow your team to quickly locate specific transaction details without manually sifting through paper records or spreadsheets.
3.  Data
3.1Overview

The Stock History feature provides a clear, chronological view of how your inventory levels have changed over time. By tracking every adjustment made to a product's stock, this feature allows you to see exactly when, where, and why inventory levels were updated, ensuring full transparency across your business operations.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.The system retrieves a complete list of all your configured store locations to ensure you can identify where stock movements are taking place.
2.The system looks up the specific history for the product you are viewing, gathering every recorded change associated with that item.
3.The system displays the collected data, showing the amount of stock added or removed, the reason for the change, and the date it occurred.
4.If no history exists for a product or no stores are configured, the system will simply show an empty list rather than an error.
3.3Key Concepts
–Stock History: A detailed log that records every increase or decrease in inventory for a specific product.
–Change Amount: The specific quantity of items that were added to or removed from your inventory.
–Reason: A descriptive note explaining why a stock adjustment occurred (e.g., a new shipment, a customer sale, or a manual correction).
–Store: The specific physical or digital location where the inventory is held and managed.
3.4Business Value

This feature is essential for maintaining accurate inventory records and operational accountability. By providing a clear audit trail, it helps you identify trends in stock consumption, investigate discrepancies, and make informed purchasing decisions. Ultimately, it reduces the time spent manually tracking inventory changes and helps prevent stockouts or overstocking by providing visibility into your supply chain movements.
