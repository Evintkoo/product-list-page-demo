1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The "Sales Order Related Documents" page provides a centralized view of all activity associated with a specific sales order. Instead of searching through different parts of the system, users can view a comprehensive history of quotations, deliveries, invoices, and returns linked to a single order on one screen. This page acts as a hub for tracking the lifecycle of a sale from the initial quote to final fulfillment or potential returns.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Authentication Check: When you open the page, the system verifies your login session. If you are not logged in, you are automatically redirected to the login screen.
2.Data Retrieval: The system identifies the specific sales order from the web address and simultaneously gathers all related records, including:

* Sales Quotations

* Delivery records

* Invoices

* Returns

3.Displaying Information: The page populates four distinct sections, each containing a table of relevant documents.
4.Navigation: You can switch between the "Details" view and this "Related Documents" view using the navigation tabs at the top of the page.
5.Drill-down Capability: Within the tables, you can click on specific document numbers or journal entries to jump directly to the detailed view of that specific record.
1.3Key Concepts
–Sales Quotation: The initial price proposal sent to a customer before the order is finalized.
–Sales Deliveries: Records tracking the physical shipment of goods, including dates and status updates.
–Sales Invoices: Financial documents detailing the amount billed, payments received, and outstanding balances.
–Sales Order Returns: Records of items sent back by the customer, including approval status and any associated refunds.
–Journal: A financial record linked to documents (like invoices or returns) that tracks the accounting impact of that transaction.
–Workflow State: The current status of a document (e.g., Draft, Approved, or Delivered), helping you understand where a document sits in the business process.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Efficiency: Staff no longer need to navigate to separate modules to find related documents; everything is linked to the original sales order.
–Enhanced Visibility: By seeing the full history—from quote to invoice and potential returns—users can quickly identify bottlenecks or discrepancies in the sales process.
–Better Customer Service: Support teams can provide immediate, accurate answers to customer inquiries regarding the status of their orders, deliveries, or billing without needing to switch screens.
–Simplified Auditing: Having all related documents grouped together makes it significantly easier to track the financial and operational trail of any given sale.
2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The Sales Order Details page provides a comprehensive view of a specific sales order. It acts as a central hub where you can see the primary order information alongside its associated history, including quotations, deliveries, invoices, and any returns. This allows staff to track the entire lifecycle of a sale from a single screen.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Authentication Check: When you open the page, the system verifies your login session. If you are not logged in, you will be automatically redirected to the login screen.
2.Load Primary Order Data: The system retrieves the core details of the sales order (such as the customer, warehouse, dates, and status) based on the order ID in the web address.
3.Parallel Data Retrieval: Once the main order is loaded, the system simultaneously fetches all related records:

* Quotations: Any price quotes associated with this order.

* Deliveries: A log of all shipments made against the order.

* Invoices: A record of all billing documents and payment statuses.

* Returns: Any items sent back by the customer.

4.Display: The retrieved information is organized into clear, readable tables and summary sections for each category, ensuring you have a complete picture of the order's progress.
2.3Key Concepts
–Sales Order: The primary document representing a customer's request for goods or services.
–Quotation: A formal document providing the customer with pricing and terms before the order is finalized.
–Delivery: A record confirming that items from the order have been shipped or handed over to the customer.
–Invoice: A financial document requesting payment for the goods or services provided.
–Return: A record of items that were sent back by the customer after delivery.
–Workflow Status: A visual indicator (such as "Draft," "Approved," or "Delivered") that shows the current stage of the order in your business process.
2.4Business Value

This feature eliminates the need to navigate between multiple screens to piece together the history of a sale. By consolidating quotations, deliveries, invoices, and returns into one view, your team can:

* Improve Customer Service: Quickly answer customer questions about their order status, billing, or returns without searching through different modules.

* Increase Efficiency: Reduce the time spent manually cross-referencing documents.

* Enhance Accuracy: Ensure that all stakeholders are looking at the same, up-to-date information regarding the order's financial and logistical status.

3.  Data
3.1Overview

The Sales Order Details page serves as a centralized hub for managing the entire lifecycle of a sales transaction. Instead of navigating through multiple screens, users can view the primary order information and access specific tabs to track related activities—such as quotations, deliveries, invoices, and returns—all in one place.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Primary Order View: When you open a Sales Order, the system displays the core details, including the customer information, delivery address, salesperson, and the current workflow status (e.g., Draft, Approved, or Delivered).
2.Itemized Breakdown: The page lists all products associated with the order, showing the quantity ordered versus the quantity delivered, along with pricing, discounts, and total amounts.
3.Quotation Tab: You can view all price quotes associated with the order, including their current status, total value, and key dates.
4.Delivery Tab: This section tracks the fulfillment process, showing when items were shipped, their current status, and the associated journal records.
5.Invoice Tab: This tab provides a financial summary, detailing the invoice date, payment status, amounts paid, and any outstanding balances.
6.Returns Tab: If any items are sent back, this section tracks the return process, including approval dates, receipt confirmation, and payment status for the return.
3.3Key Concepts
–Sales Order: The master record that captures the agreement between the business and the customer, containing all items, pricing, and shipping details.
–Workflow Status: A visual indicator that shows the current stage of the order (e.g., whether it is still being drafted, has been approved, or is currently out for delivery).
–Tabbed Interface: A design approach that organizes related documents (Quotations, Deliveries, Invoices, Returns) into separate, easy-to-access sections within the same page.
–Journal: A record-keeping feature that tracks the financial or inventory movements associated with a specific transaction, such as a delivery or an invoice.
3.4Business Value

This feature significantly improves operational efficiency by providing a "single source of truth" for any given sales order. By consolidating quotations, fulfillment, billing, and returns into one view, staff can quickly answer customer inquiries, resolve discrepancies, and monitor the financial health of an order without switching between different modules or reports.
