1.  Data
1.1Overview

This feature establishes a standardized way for our Mini ERP system to organize and retrieve warehouse stock information. By defining a strict structure for how stock data is packaged, we ensure that information—such as product names, warehouse locations, and various inventory counts—is always consistent, accurate, and ready to be used by other parts of the application.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Define the Data Structure: The system establishes a formal blueprint (schema) that dictates exactly what information must be included for every stock record, such as product identifiers, warehouse names, and specific quantity types.
2.Retrieve the Data: The system runs a query against the warehouse database to pull the requested stock information, ensuring that all eight required data points are collected for every item.
3.Validate and Prepare: The system verifies that the retrieved data matches the established blueprint (e.g., ensuring quantities are recorded as numbers) so that the information is clean and ready for the system to use.
1.3Key Concepts
–Schema: A standardized blueprint that defines the required fields and data types, ensuring consistency across the system.
–Inventory Quantity: The total amount of a product currently held in a specific warehouse.
–Reserved Quantity: Stock that has been set aside for specific customer orders but has not yet left the warehouse.
–Consignment Quantity: Stock held at a warehouse that is owned by a third party but managed by us.
–Available Quantity: The actual amount of stock ready for new orders, calculated by accounting for reserved and consignment items.
1.4Business Value

This feature provides a "single source of truth" for inventory levels. By enforcing a strict data structure, we reduce the risk of errors when reporting stock levels, prevent data mismatches between different parts of the ERP, and ensure that warehouse managers always have an accurate view of what is available to sell versus what is already spoken for.
