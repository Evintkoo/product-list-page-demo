1.1Overview

The Service feature in the Lumi ERP system provides a structured way to manage workshop activities. It uses two primary documents to track the lifecycle of a service request: the Service Order (the high-level administrative record) and the Service Work Order (the operational document used by mechanics to perform the actual work). Together, these tables ensure that every service job is properly documented, linked to the correct customer and vehicle, and tracked through its various stages of completion.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initiation: A Service Order is created to act as the master record for a customer's request. This includes details like the type of service, the assigned advisor, and the vehicle involved.
2.Operational Planning: A Service Work Order is generated to serve as the specific job card for the workshop. This document tracks the progress of the work, from initial scheduling to final completion.
3.Linking: The Service Work Order is linked back to the original Service Order, ensuring that administrative and operational data remain synchronized.
4.Inventory Integration: If the work requires parts that are not currently in stock at the local store, the system identifies the shortfall and can automatically trigger a request for those parts from a central warehouse.
5.Status Tracking: Throughout the process, both documents are updated to reflect their current status (e.g., draft, in-progress, or completed), allowing managers to monitor the status of any job at a glance.
1.3Key Concepts
–Service Order: The primary administrative header that captures the "big picture" of a service request, including financial totals, tax information, and customer details.
–Service Work Order: The detailed, day-to-day operational document that mechanics use to perform tasks on a vehicle.
–Reference Code: A unique, system-generated identifier for every order and work order, ensuring that every job can be easily tracked and audited.
–Status/State: A tracking mechanism that defines where a job is in its lifecycle (e.g., waiting, in-progress, or completed), which helps in managing workflow and reporting.
–Stock Order Integration: A smart feature that automatically detects when parts are missing for a job and initiates a request to replenish inventory, preventing delays in service.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Operational Efficiency: By separating the administrative "Service Order" from the mechanical "Work Order," the system allows office staff and workshop technicians to focus on their respective tasks without data clutter.
–Better Inventory Control: The automated link between missing parts and stock requests reduces manual effort and minimizes the risk of service delays caused by unavailable spare parts.
–Enhanced Accuracy: Centralizing data like vehicle history, odometer readings, and service status reduces human error and provides a reliable audit trail for every vehicle serviced.
–Streamlined Workflow: Real-time status tracking ensures that service advisors and managers always know the exact progress of a job, leading to better communication with customers and more predictable workshop turnaround times.
