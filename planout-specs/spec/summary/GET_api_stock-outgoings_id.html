1.  Logic
1.1Overview

This feature provides a way to retrieve the full details of a specific "Stock Outgoing" document. A Stock Outgoing document tracks items leaving your inventory. By using this feature, you can view the primary document information alongside all associated items (lines), related production tasks (work orders), and the original requests that triggered the outgoing movement (stock orders).

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Verification: The system identifies the user's company based on their current session to ensure they only access data they are authorized to see.
2.Document Lookup: The system searches for the specific Stock Outgoing document requested. If it cannot be found or does not belong to the user's company, an error is returned.
3.Data Gathering: Once the document is located, the system automatically collects all related information, including the list of items being moved, any linked work orders, and any associated stock orders.
4.Consolidation: The system combines the main document details with all the gathered related information into a single, comprehensive report.
5.Delivery: The complete package of information is presented to the user.
1.3Key Concepts
–Stock Outgoing: A formal record representing inventory items that are being removed from your stock.
–Lines: The individual items or products included within a specific stock outgoing document.
–Work Orders: Production or maintenance tasks that are linked to the movement of these goods.
–Stock Orders: The original requests or source documents that initiated the need for this stock outgoing.
–Company Scope: A security measure that ensures users can only view documents that belong to their specific organization.
–Approval Status: Certain financial details (such as journal references) are kept blank until the document has been officially approved.
1.4Business Value

This feature provides a "single source of truth" for inventory movements. By bundling the document with its related work and stock orders, staff can quickly audit why items were removed, what tasks were performed in relation to that movement, and which original orders triggered the process. This reduces the need to manually cross-reference multiple screens or reports, saving time and improving accuracy in inventory management.

2.  Data
2.1Overview

This feature provides a way to retrieve the full details of a specific "Stock Outgoing" record. A Stock Outgoing represents a movement of inventory leaving your facility. By using this feature, the system gathers the main document information along with all associated line items, linked work orders, and stock orders into a single, comprehensive view. This ensures that users have a complete picture of the inventory transaction and its origins.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Verification: The system identifies the specific record requested and ensures it belongs to your company.
2.Document Retrieval: The system fetches the primary details of the Stock Outgoing record (such as its status and creation date).
3.Line Item Collection: The system gathers all individual items (lines) associated with that specific outgoing shipment.
4.Relationship Mapping: The system identifies and attaches any related Work Orders and Stock Orders that triggered or are linked to this movement.
5.Consolidation: The system combines all this information into one complete package and presents it to the user.
2.3Key Concepts
–Stock Outgoing: A record tracking inventory that has been removed from your stock.
–Company Scoping: A security measure that ensures users can only view records belonging to their own organization.
–Linked Entities: Related documents (like Work Orders or Stock Orders) that provide context on why the inventory was moved.
–Source Line Information: Details attached to items that indicate if they were generated by another process; this helps the system determine if a record should be editable or if it is locked for review.
2.4Business Value

This feature simplifies inventory management by providing a "single source of truth" for any outgoing shipment. Instead of searching through multiple areas of the system, staff can instantly see the full history and context of a stock movement. This transparency helps teams quickly verify why inventory was moved, identify which work orders were fulfilled, and understand the status of the transaction, ultimately leading to more accurate inventory reporting and faster troubleshooting.
