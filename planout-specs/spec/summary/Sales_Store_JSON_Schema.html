1.  LOGIC
1.1Overview

The Sales Store feature provides a structured way for the Lumi ERP system to manage and retrieve point-of-sale (POS) transaction data. It allows the system to list sales, view detailed information for a specific transaction, and record new sales. The feature ensures that data is consistently organized, filtered, and paginated, making it easier for store staff to track sales performance and manage customer records.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Listing Sales: When viewing the sales list, the system automatically filters for POS-originated transactions and excludes any voided sales. Users can further refine this list using optional filters like date ranges, store location, salesperson, or customer name. The system returns a paginated list of sales, including key details like the store name and total amount.
2.Viewing Sale Details: When a user selects a specific sale, the system retrieves the full record. This includes the primary sale information, a breakdown of all items purchased (such as products and quantities), and the payment methods used for that transaction.
3.Creating a New Sale: When a new sale is processed, the system captures the core transaction data, the list of items sold, and the payment details. These are saved together as a single, unified record to ensure the financial and inventory data remains accurate.
1.3Key Concepts
–Pagination: A method of breaking large lists of sales into smaller, manageable pages to improve system performance and readability.
–Workflow State: The current status of a sale (e.g., draft, pending, completed, or voided), which determines how the sale is displayed and processed.
–Source: A label identifying where the sale originated (e.g., POS, ecommerce, or other), ensuring that store-specific reports only include relevant data.
–Denormalization: The practice of including descriptive information (like "Store Name" or "Customer Name") directly within the sale record, so the system doesn't have to look up those details from other areas every time a sale is viewed.
–Transaction: A safety feature that ensures all parts of a new sale—the main record, the items, and the payments—are saved successfully together; if any part fails, the entire sale is rolled back to prevent incomplete data.
1.4Business Value
–Operational Efficiency: By providing a dedicated view for POS sales with built-in filtering and search, staff can quickly locate specific transactions without manual sorting.
–Data Integrity: The use of standardized schemas ensures that every sale record is complete and consistent, reducing errors in financial reporting and inventory tracking.
–Improved Customer Service: By linking customer and vehicle information directly to sales, staff can easily access history and provide more personalized service at the point of sale.
–Scalability: The structured approach to handling sales data allows the business to grow across multiple store locations while maintaining a unified and reliable reporting process.
2.  DATA
2.1Overview

This feature establishes a standardized "blueprint" (JSON Schema) for how the Lumi ERP system handles sales data. By defining exactly what information is required and how it should be formatted, we ensure that the system consistently records and displays sales information across the platform. This covers both viewing a list of store sales and creating new sales records.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Data Retrieval (Viewing Sales): When a user views the "Store Sales" list, the system pulls data from the central sales database. It combines this with store information to provide a clear, readable list of transactions.
2.Standardized Formatting: The system applies a strict structure to this data, ensuring that every sale includes essential details like the document number, date, customer name, vehicle registration, status, and total amount.
3.Pagination: To keep the interface fast and responsive, the system organizes the sales list into manageable pages, providing the total count of records to help the user navigate through the history.
4.Data Submission (Creating Sales): When a new sale is created, the system validates the incoming information against the required blueprint. This ensures that the sale, its individual items, and the payment details are all captured correctly before being saved to the database.
2.3Key Concepts
–JSON Schema: A standardized set of rules that defines the "shape" of data, ensuring that information sent to or received from the system is complete and correctly formatted.
–Sale Entity: The core record representing a transaction, containing high-level details like the store, salesperson, and total amount.
–Sale Items: The individual products or services included within a single sale, tracking quantities, prices, and taxes for each line item.
–Sale Payments: The financial breakdown of how a sale was settled, including the payment method (e.g., cash, card) and the specific amount paid.
–Workflow State: The current status of a sale (e.g., draft, pending, completed, or voided), which determines how the sale is displayed and handled in the system.
2.4Business Value
–Data Integrity: By enforcing a strict structure, we prevent errors and missing information, ensuring that financial and operational reports are always accurate.
–System Reliability: Standardized schemas allow different parts of the software to "talk" to each other reliably, reducing bugs and unexpected behavior when updating or viewing sales.
–Improved User Experience: Consistent data formatting ensures that the sales list is always easy to read, searchable, and navigable, allowing staff to quickly find the information they need.
–Scalability: A clear blueprint makes it easier for the development team to add new features or integrations in the future without disrupting existing sales processes.
