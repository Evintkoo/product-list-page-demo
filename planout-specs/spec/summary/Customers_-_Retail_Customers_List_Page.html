1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The Retail Customers List page provides a centralized dashboard for managing your retail customer base. It allows you to view a comprehensive list of customers, search for specific individuals using various criteria, and navigate to detailed customer profiles or create new entries.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initial Load: When you open the page, the system automatically retrieves and displays all retail customers.
2.Filtering: You can narrow down the list by entering information into the filter bar (such as Name, Phone, Tax Number, or Customer Group).
3.Search Execution: Once you have entered your desired criteria, click the "Apply Search" button. The system will refresh the table to show only the customers that match your specific filters.
4.Navigation: You can switch between different customer categories (Retail, Wholesale, or Franchisee) using the navigation tabs at the top of the page.
5.Customer Management:
–Click on any row in the table to view the full details for that specific customer.
–Click the "Add New Retail Customer" button to create a new record.
–Use the "Deactivate" button within the table to open a confirmation window for removing a customer's active status.
1.3Key Concepts
–Filter Bar: A set of input fields that allows you to refine the customer list based on specific data points like status, contact information, or location.
–Retail Customer Type: The system specifically targets retail-type customers for this view, ensuring you are only seeing the relevant segment of your database.
–Data Table: The main display area that organizes customer information into columns (such as ID, Name, Email, and Store) for easy readability.
–Sorting: You can click on specific column headers (like Name or Code) to organize the list alphabetically or numerically to find information faster.
–Deactivation: A safety feature that requires a confirmation step before changing a customer's status to "inactive," preventing accidental updates.
1.4Business Value

This feature streamlines daily operations by providing a fast, searchable interface for customer data. By allowing staff to quickly filter by specific attributes—such as tax numbers or store locations—the system reduces the time spent searching for records. Additionally, the ability to easily navigate between customer types and manage status updates directly from the list page improves overall data accuracy and administrative efficiency.

2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The Retail Customers List page is designed to provide a centralized view for managing retail customer data. This feature allows users to browse, search, and filter customer records efficiently. By using specific criteria—such as customer groups, regions, or general search terms—users can quickly locate the information they need, which is then displayed in a clear, organized table format.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initiate Request: When the page loads or when a user applies search/filter criteria, the system sends a request to retrieve the relevant retail customer data.
2.Apply Filters: The system includes the user's selected filters (such as search text, customer group, region, or page number) to ensure the results are specific to the user's needs.
3.Retrieve Data: The system fetches the customer information from the database based on the provided criteria.
4.Format Data: The raw data is processed and mapped into a user-friendly table structure, ensuring that details like contact information, store location, and account status are correctly aligned with the table columns.
5.Display Results: The final, formatted list is rendered on the screen, allowing the user to view the updated customer information.
2.3Key Concepts
–Filters: Tools that allow users to narrow down the customer list by specific categories like region, group, or keyword searches.
–Pagination: A method of breaking the customer list into smaller, manageable pages to improve performance and readability.
–Data Mapping: The process of taking raw system information and organizing it into the specific columns shown in the table (e.g., ensuring the "email" field appears in the "Email" column).
–Customer Status: A visual indicator or action that shows whether a customer account is currently active.
2.4Business Value

This feature streamlines daily operations by reducing the time spent searching for customer records. By providing robust filtering and a clear, structured view, staff can quickly access accurate customer details, improve response times for customer inquiries, and maintain better oversight of the retail customer base.

3.  Data
3.1Overview

The Retail Customers List page is a centralized dashboard designed to help you efficiently manage and view your customer base. It provides a clear, organized view of all retail customers, allowing you to quickly locate specific individuals or groups using flexible search and filtering tools. The page is designed to handle large volumes of data by breaking the list into manageable pages, ensuring the system remains fast and easy to navigate.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Define Search and Filters: You enter your criteria, such as a customer's name, email, or unique code. You can also narrow down the list by selecting specific customer groups, geographic regions, or by toggling between active and inactive customer statuses.
2.Data Retrieval: The system processes your request, applying these filters to the database to find only the relevant records.
3.Pagination: The system organizes the results into pages (e.g., 20 or 50 customers per page) so you aren't overwhelmed by a single long list.
4.Display: The filtered data is presented in a clean table format, showing key details like contact information, assigned store, and current status for each customer.
3.3Key Concepts
–Pagination: A method of splitting a large list of data into smaller, separate pages to improve performance and readability.
–Filtering: The ability to narrow down the list of customers based on specific attributes like their region, group, or active status.
–Search Query: A text-based search that scans customer names, emails, and codes to find a specific match instantly.
–Customer Status: A toggleable indicator that identifies whether a customer is currently active or inactive within the system.
3.4Business Value
–Improved Productivity: Staff can quickly find the information they need without scrolling through thousands of records.
–Better Data Organization: By grouping and filtering customers by region or status, managers can gain clearer insights into their customer base.
–Scalability: The use of pagination ensures that the system remains responsive and fast, even as your customer list grows over time.
–Accuracy: Standardized mapping of customer details ensures that every user sees consistent, reliable information across the entire platform.
