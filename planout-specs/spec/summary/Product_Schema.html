1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Product Schema feature provides a standardized way for our system to organize, retrieve, and display product information. It acts as a "blueprint" that ensures all product data—including basic details, brand information, product types, and stock levels across different warehouses and stores—is consistent and easy to access. Whether you are viewing a list of products or looking at the history of a specific item, this feature ensures the information is structured reliably.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Defining the Structure: The system establishes standard formats (schemas) for product lists and individual product details, ensuring essential information like the SKU, name, and brand are always present.
2.Gathering Inventory Data: The system queries warehouse and store stock records to understand current inventory levels.
3.Consolidating Information: The system performs a "consolidated query," which pulls together the core product details, brand, type, and stock quantities into a single, unified view.
4.Enriching with Context: The system joins this consolidated data with specific records, such as brand descriptions, product categories, and store-specific stock counts.
5.Tracking History: The system maintains a history log for each product, recording changes to pricing, status, and other key attributes over time.
6.Delivering the Response: The final, organized data is presented in a consistent format, ready to be used by the ERP interface or other system modules.
1.3Key Concepts
–Schema: A set of rules that defines how data must be organized, ensuring that every product record has the same fields and structure.
–Consolidated Query: A process that combines data from multiple sources (like warehouse stock, store stock, and brand info) into one complete record.
–Inventory Tracking: The ability to see exactly how much of a product is available in specific warehouses and retail stores.
–Product History: A chronological log of changes made to a product, such as price updates or status changes, which helps with auditing and tracking.
–Pagination: A method used when displaying lists of products to break them into manageable pages, improving system performance and user experience.
1.4Business Value
–Data Consistency: By enforcing a strict structure, we eliminate errors caused by missing or mismatched information, ensuring that reports and inventory counts are always accurate.
–Operational Efficiency: Staff can quickly access a "single source of truth" for any product, reducing the time spent searching through different systems to find stock levels or pricing history.
–Better Decision Making: With real-time visibility into stock across all locations and a clear history of product changes, management can make more informed decisions regarding purchasing, pricing, and inventory management.
–Scalability: The standardized schema allows the system to grow easily, making it simple to add new product types or warehouse locations without disrupting existing operations.
