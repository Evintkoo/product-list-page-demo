1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Product Schema feature provides a unified way to access and manage comprehensive product information within the ERP system. Instead of pulling data from multiple disconnected sources, this feature consolidates product details—including store-specific stock, warehouse inventory, branding, and categorization—into a single, structured record. This ensures that whenever you view, create, or update a product, you are working with a complete and consistent set of data.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Request Initiation: When a user requests product details (or performs a search), the system triggers a consolidated data query.
2.Data Aggregation: The system automatically joins information from several areas:

* Core Product Data: Basic details like name, SKU, and description.

* Categorization: Links to specific product brands and types.

* Inventory Levels: Real-time stock counts from both store locations and warehouses.

* Metadata: Associated tags and historical audit logs.

3.Calculation: The system calculates "available quantity" by subtracting reserved and consignment stock from the total inventory on hand.
4.Response Formatting: The gathered data is structured into a standardized JSON format, ensuring the information is ready for the user interface or other system processes.
5.Pagination: For lists of products, the system applies limits and offsets to ensure the application remains fast and responsive, even with large catalogs.
1.3Key Concepts
–Consolidated Query: A single database operation that merges information from multiple tables (like brands, types, and inventory) to provide a "360-degree view" of a product.
–Available Quantity: A calculated value representing stock that is truly ready for sale, derived by taking total inventory and removing items that are reserved or held on consignment.
–SKU (Stock Keeping Unit): The unique identifier used to track specific products across all stores and warehouses.
–Audit Trail: A historical record of changes made to a product, such as price updates or status changes, which allows for tracking and accountability.
–Pagination: A method of breaking large lists of products into smaller, manageable pages to improve system performance and user experience.
1.4Business Value
–Single Source of Truth: By consolidating data, you eliminate discrepancies between store stock and warehouse inventory, ensuring everyone sees the same accurate numbers.
–Improved Decision Making: With instant access to pricing, stock levels, and product history, managers can make faster, data-driven decisions regarding inventory replenishment and sales strategies.
–Operational Efficiency: The system handles complex calculations (like available stock) automatically, reducing manual work and the risk of human error.
–Scalability: The use of pagination and structured data ensures that the system remains fast and reliable as your product catalog and store count grow.
