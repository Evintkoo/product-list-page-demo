1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Product Schema feature provides a unified way to access and manage comprehensive product information within the ERP system. Instead of looking at fragmented data, this feature consolidates core product details—such as pricing, branding, and categorization—with real-time inventory levels across stores and warehouses. It also includes audit capabilities to track historical changes to product settings and pricing.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Consolidated Data Retrieval: When you request product details, the system performs a single, efficient operation that joins core product information with its associated brand, type, tags, and current stock levels in both stores and warehouses.
2.Inventory Calculation: The system automatically calculates the "available" stock by taking the total inventory and subtracting items that are currently reserved or held on consignment.
3.Historical Audit: If you need to review past changes, the system retrieves a chronological log of updates for a specific product, including previous pricing, tax status, and configuration settings.
4.Warehouse-Specific Analysis: For deeper inventory management, the system can pull detailed stock reports for specific warehouses, including cost analysis and transit status, filtered by your specific needs.
5.Pagination: To ensure the system remains fast and responsive, all lists of products or history logs are returned in manageable chunks using pagination (limiting the number of items per page).
1.3Key Concepts
–Consolidated Record: A single view that combines data from multiple sources (products, brands, types, and stock locations) so you don't have to manually piece information together.
–Available Quantity: The actual stock ready for sale, calculated by excluding reserved items and consignment stock from the total inventory.
–Audit Log: A historical record of changes made to a product, allowing you to see who changed a price or setting and when it happened.
–Pagination: A method of breaking large amounts of data into smaller, numbered pages to improve performance and readability.
–SKU (Stock Keeping Unit): The unique identifier used to track specific products across all store and warehouse locations.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Decision Making: By having a single, accurate view of product data and real-time stock levels, managers can make faster, more informed decisions about pricing and inventory replenishment.
–Operational Efficiency: Consolidating multiple data points into one request reduces the time and effort required to generate reports or view product status.
–Enhanced Accountability: The historical audit log provides transparency, ensuring that all changes to product pricing or status are tracked and can be reviewed for accuracy.
–Better Inventory Control: With clear visibility into reserved and available stock, the business can prevent overselling and optimize stock distribution across different locations.
