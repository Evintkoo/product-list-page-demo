1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Product Schema feature provides a standardized way for our ERP system to organize, retrieve, and display product information. It acts as a "blueprint" that ensures every product record—whether you are viewing a list, checking specific details, or looking at stock levels—contains consistent and accurate data. This feature connects core product information with related details like warehouse inventory, store-specific stock, brand categories, and historical pricing changes.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Product Listing: The system generates a paginated list of products, ensuring that even if no products exist, the system returns a clean, empty response rather than an error.
2.Detailed Retrieval: When a specific product is selected, the system validates the record against a strict set of requirements (such as ensuring a SKU, name, and brand are present).
3.Consolidated Data Gathering: The system performs a comprehensive search that pulls together all related information—including warehouse stock, store-specific inventory, brand metadata, and product types—into a single, unified view.
4.Inventory & History Integration: The system calculates real-time stock availability (accounting for reserved and in-transit items) and retrieves the product’s history, such as past price adjustments or status changes.
5.Validation: Throughout the process, the system checks all data against predefined schemas to ensure that numbers, dates, and text fields are formatted correctly before they are displayed to the user.
1.3Key Concepts
–Product Schema: The master template that defines exactly what information must be included for every product (e.g., SKU, name, pricing, and status).
–Consolidated Query: A high-efficiency process that gathers data from multiple different areas (like warehouses, stores, and brands) at once, rather than making several separate requests.
–Paginated Response: A method of displaying large amounts of data in smaller, manageable chunks (pages) to keep the system fast and easy to navigate.
–Inventory Availability: A calculation that determines how many items are actually ready for sale by subtracting reserved or in-transit stock from the total inventory.
–Validation: An automated check that ensures data is complete and accurate, preventing errors like missing names or invalid pricing.
1.4Business Value
–Data Consistency: By enforcing a strict schema, we ensure that every product record is complete and reliable, reducing errors in reporting and inventory management.
–Improved Performance: The consolidated query approach reduces the load on our database, leading to faster load times when viewing product details.
–Operational Clarity: Staff can instantly see accurate, real-time stock levels across different warehouses and stores, which helps prevent overselling and improves supply chain planning.
–Auditability: By maintaining a structured history of product changes, the business can easily track pricing updates and status modifications for better accountability.
