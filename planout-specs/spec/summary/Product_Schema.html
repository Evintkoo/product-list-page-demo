1.  Data
1.1Overview

This feature defines the complete "Product" record that the system uses whenever someone looks up, creates, edits, or changes the status of a product in the ERP. Rather than just storing basic product details, the system pulls together information from multiple related areas — pricing, stock levels across stores and warehouses, brand and category details, tags, and historical changes — so that anyone viewing a product gets a full, accurate picture in one place.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Product List Request: When someone requests a list of products (for example, browsing a product catalog), the system retrieves matching products along with a total count, and returns them in manageable pages rather than all at once.
2.Single Product Lookup: When someone requests full details for one specific product at a specific store, the system gathers information from several sources at once: the store's stock on hand, the core product details, any tags attached to the product, warehouse inventory figures, the brand information, and the product type/category information.
3.Combining the Data: All of this gathered information is merged into one consolidated view, which is then cleaned up and reshaped into a single, easy-to-read Product record before being shown to the user.
4.Warehouse Stock Detail (Simple View): For a quick look at how much stock exists across warehouses for one product, the system can pull a simplified, ready-made stock summary.
5.Warehouse Stock Detail (Full Calculation): For a more detailed and accurate warehouse stock view, the system calculates real-time figures: how much inventory exists, how much is reserved for pending orders, how much is on its way in (in-transit), and how much is actually available to sell or move — along with cost information.
6.Product Change History: When someone wants to see how a product's pricing or status has changed over time, the system retrieves a chronological log showing what changed, when, and who made the change.
1.3Key Concepts
–Product Record: The master set of information about a product — its name, brand, category, pricing, and status flags (such as whether it's active or price can be adjusted locally).
–Store Stock: How much of a product is currently available at a specific physical store.
–Warehouse Stock: How much of a product exists in a warehouse, further broken down into what's reserved, what's incoming, and what's actually available.
–Reserved Quantity: Stock that has already been promised to an order and can't be sold again until that order is resolved.
–In-Transit Quantity: Stock that has been shipped but hasn't yet arrived at its destination warehouse.
–Tags: Optional labels attached to a product to help with searching, filtering, or grouping.
–Brand & Type: Reference information that categorizes a product by manufacturer (brand) and product category (type).
–Product History: A running log that records every meaningful change made to a product's price or status, including who made the change and when — useful for audits and troubleshooting.
–Pagination: A way of splitting large result sets (like long product lists) into smaller, manageable pages instead of loading everything at once.
1.4Business Value

This feature ensures that everyone working with product data — whether in a store, warehouse, or head office — sees one consistent, trustworthy version of the truth about each product. By combining pricing, stock, and categorization data into a single view, it reduces the risk of staff working with outdated or incomplete information, speeds up decision-making (such as whether to restock or promote a product), and provides an audit trail for accountability when prices or statuses change. It also supports scalability, since large product catalogs and multi-warehouse operations can be handled efficiently through pagination and real-time stock calculations.
