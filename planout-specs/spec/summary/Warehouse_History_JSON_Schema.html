1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Warehouse History feature provides a structured way for the Lumi ERP system to track and display the movement of stock for specific products. It allows the system to record every change in quantity—such as deliveries, orders, or balance adjustments—and provides a clear, organized history of these events. Additionally, the feature includes a helper function to retrieve a list of all available warehouses, ensuring users can easily select the correct location when viewing or managing stock data.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Warehouse Selection: The system retrieves a complete list of all warehouses, including their unique IDs and names, to populate selection menus in the user interface.
2.Product History Request: When a user selects a specific product, the system requests its stock history.
3.Data Filtering and Sorting: The system searches the database for all records associated with that specific product. It automatically sorts these records by date, showing the most recent changes first.
4.Pagination: To ensure the system remains fast and responsive, the history is delivered in manageable chunks (pages) rather than loading all records at once.
5.Display: The retrieved data is presented in a standardized format, showing details like the type of transaction, the quantity changed, who made the change, and any associated notes.
1.3Key Concepts
–Stock History: A chronological log of every event that impacts the quantity of a product in a warehouse (e.g., a new delivery or a completed sales order).
–Transaction Type: A category assigned to each stock change, such as "Purchase Delivery" or "Sales Delivery," which helps users quickly identify the nature of the movement.
–Pagination: A method of breaking large amounts of data into smaller, easier-to-read pages to improve system performance.
–Reference Code: An optional identifier (like an invoice or order number) linked to a stock change to help with auditing and cross-referencing.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Accountability: By tracking who created a record and when, the business gains full visibility into stock adjustments, reducing errors and unauthorized changes.
–Enhanced Auditability: The inclusion of transaction types and reference codes makes it significantly easier for staff to reconcile inventory levels and investigate discrepancies.
–Better Decision Making: With a clear, sorted history of stock movements, managers can identify trends in product demand and warehouse efficiency.
–User Efficiency: Standardizing how warehouse and stock data is retrieved ensures that the interface remains consistent and fast, allowing staff to find the information they need without unnecessary delays.
