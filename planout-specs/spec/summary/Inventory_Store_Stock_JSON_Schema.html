1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Inventory Store Stock feature provides a structured way to manage and track inventory movements between warehouses and stores within the Lumi ERP system. It covers three primary operational areas: Stock Orders (transferring goods), Stock Returns (sending items back from stores), and Stock Opnames (physical stock counts and adjustments). This system ensures that inventory data remains accurate, auditable, and synchronized across all locations.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Stock Orders:

* Users initiate a transfer request from a warehouse to a store.

* The system tracks the order through various stages: Draft, Confirmed, Sent, and Received.

* The system maintains a detailed list of items per order, including quantities ordered, sent, and received, along with any specific remarks from the warehouse or store.

2.Stock Returns:

* Stores can initiate a return document to send items back to a warehouse or supplier.

* The system tracks the return from creation through approval, ensuring that returned stock is properly accounted for in the destination location.

3.Stock Opnames (Physical Counts):

* Stores perform physical stock counts to verify system records against actual inventory.

* The system generates a document that compares "system quantity" with "counted quantity."

* Any discrepancies (variance) are calculated, and the system supports a workflow to confirm and approve these adjustments to reconcile the inventory.

1.3Key Concepts
–Stock Order: A formal request to move inventory from a warehouse to a specific store.
–Stock Return: A document used to process the movement of goods from a store back to a warehouse or supplier.
–Stock Opname: A physical inventory audit process used to identify and correct discrepancies between recorded stock and actual stock on hand.
–Workflow State: The current status of a document (e.g., Draft, Confirmed, Approved, Completed, Cancelled), which dictates what actions can be performed next.
–Source Type: Identifies the origin of a stock request, such as a standard stock order, a sale, or a service work order, allowing the system to link inventory movements to specific business activities.
–Variance: The difference between the expected system quantity and the actual counted quantity during a stock opname.
1.4Business Value

* Inventory Accuracy: By formalizing stock counts (Opnames) and transfers (Orders/Returns), the system minimizes discrepancies and ensures that store stock levels are always reliable.

* Operational Efficiency: The automated tracking of document lifecycles reduces manual paperwork and provides clear visibility into where inventory is at any given time.

* Auditability: Every movement and adjustment is tied to a specific document, user, and timestamp, creating a clear trail for financial and operational reporting.

* Seamless Integration: By linking stock orders to other business processes (like service orders or sales), the system ensures that inventory consumption is automatically reflected in the ERP, preventing stockouts and overstocking.
