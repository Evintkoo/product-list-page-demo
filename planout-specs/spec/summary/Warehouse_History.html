1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The Warehouse History feature provides a clear, searchable log of all stock movements for a specific product. It allows users to see exactly how and when inventory levels have changed across different warehouse locations, helping to maintain accurate stock records and improve accountability.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Automatic Loading: When you open the product history page, the system automatically retrieves and displays the full list of stock transactions for that product.
2.Applying Filters: You can narrow down the list using the filter bar at the top of the screen. You can filter by:

* Warehouse: Select a specific location or view all.

* Transaction Type: Filter by the nature of the movement (e.g., deliveries or imports).

* Date Range: Specify a "From" and "To" date to view activity within a certain timeframe.

* User: Search for records created by a specific person or role.

3.Search Execution: After adjusting your filters, click the "Search" button to refresh the table with the relevant data.
4.Reviewing Details: The table displays the history in chronological order, showing the date, warehouse, transaction type, the amount of stock added or removed, any relevant notes, and the user who recorded the change.
1.3Key Concepts
–Stock Activity: A record of any event that increases or decreases the quantity of a product in a warehouse.
–Transaction Type: The category of the movement, which helps identify if the change was due to a purchase, a sale, or an internal adjustment.
–Quantity Changed: The specific number of items added (positive) or removed (negative) during a transaction.
–Filter Bar: A set of controls that allows you to refine the displayed data to find specific information quickly.
1.4Business Value
–Improved Accountability: By tracking who created each record and including descriptive notes, the system ensures there is a clear audit trail for every inventory change.
–Operational Transparency: Warehouse managers can quickly investigate stock discrepancies by reviewing the history of specific products across different locations.
–Efficient Reporting: The ability to filter by date and transaction type allows staff to generate quick insights into stock movement trends without needing to manually sort through large amounts of data.
2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The Warehouse History feature provides a transparent audit trail for your inventory. By viewing the history of a specific product, you can see exactly how its stock levels have changed over time across different warehouse locations. This feature ensures that every adjustment to your inventory is recorded, helping you maintain accurate stock counts and accountability.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Trigger: When you open the product details page, the system automatically initiates a request to retrieve the stock history for that specific item.
2.Data Retrieval: The system searches the database for all recorded activities associated with that product, organizing them from the most recent to the oldest.
3.Formatting: The system gathers the relevant details for each activity—such as the date, the warehouse involved, the type of change, the quantity adjusted, and the user responsible.
4.Display: This information is mapped directly to your screen, presenting a clear, chronological list of all stock movements for the product.
2.3Key Concepts
–Stock Activity: A record of any event that changes the quantity of a product, such as incoming shipments or outgoing orders.
–Change Type: A label describing the nature of the inventory adjustment (e.g., restock, sale, or manual correction).
–Quantity Changed: The specific number of units added to or removed from the warehouse inventory during a single transaction.
–Audit Trail: The collection of historical data that allows you to track who performed an action and when it occurred.
2.4Business Value

This feature significantly improves inventory management by providing full visibility into stock fluctuations. By tracking these changes, your team can quickly identify the source of inventory discrepancies, verify the accuracy of warehouse operations, and maintain a reliable record of product movement. This level of detail is essential for troubleshooting stock issues and ensuring that your inventory data remains trustworthy for daily operations and reporting.

3.  Data
3.1Overview

The Warehouse History feature provides a comprehensive audit trail for all stock movements within the Lumi ERP system. It automatically records every change in inventory levels—whether caused by customer orders, internal transfers, or manual adjustments—and stores the details in a permanent log. This allows your team to view the complete lifecycle of a product’s stock levels across different warehouse locations.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Automatic Recording: Whenever a stock level is adjusted, the system automatically captures the details of the change, including the product involved, the specific warehouse, the amount of the change, and who performed the action.
2.Data Storage: The system saves the "before" and "after" quantities, the reason for the change, and a timestamp, creating a reliable record of the event.
3.Product-Level Auditing: Users can view a chronological history for a specific product, showing exactly how its stock has moved across various warehouses over time.
4.Warehouse-Level Auditing: Users can view a chronological history for a specific warehouse, showing all stock activity for every product stored at that location.
3.3Key Concepts
–Stock Movement: Any event that results in a change to the quantity of a product, such as receiving new inventory, shipping an order, or correcting a count.
–Audit Trail: A secure, chronological record that tracks the history of changes, providing transparency and accountability for inventory management.
–Quantity Delta: The specific amount by which the stock increased or decreased during a single transaction.
–Reference ID: A link to the original transaction (such as an order number) that triggered the stock change, allowing for easy cross-referencing.
3.4Business Value
–Improved Accountability: By logging which user performed a stock adjustment and when, you can easily identify the source of any inventory discrepancies.
–Enhanced Troubleshooting: If stock levels appear incorrect, staff can review the history log to see exactly when and why the numbers changed, significantly reducing time spent on manual investigations.
–Operational Transparency: Managers gain clear visibility into how products are moving through the supply chain, helping to identify trends and optimize warehouse performance.
–Regulatory Compliance: Maintaining a detailed history of inventory changes is essential for accurate reporting and meeting standard business auditing requirements.
