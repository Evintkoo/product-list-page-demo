1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Purchase History feature establishes a standardized digital record for every product procurement within our Mini ERP system. By defining a strict structure for this data, we ensure that every purchase entry—from the date and supplier to the final cost breakdown—is captured consistently. This creates a reliable foundation for tracking inventory costs and supplier performance.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Data Definition: We define a formal blueprint (schema) that dictates exactly what information must be included for every purchase record, such as the product, quantity, pricing, and tax details.
2.Validation: Before any purchase data is accepted into the system, it is checked against this blueprint. If any required information is missing or formatted incorrectly, the system rejects the entry to prevent data errors.
3.Database Storage: Once validated, the information is organized into the Purchase History table, ensuring that all financial and logistical data is stored in a structured, permanent format for future reporting.
1.3Key Concepts
–Schema: A set of rules that defines the structure of our data, ensuring that every purchase record contains the same necessary fields.
–Purchase Record: A single entry in our system that captures the full details of a specific transaction, including the product, supplier, and financial totals.
–Data Integrity: The process of ensuring that only complete and correctly formatted information is saved, which prevents "dirty data" from entering our reports.
–Financial Breakdown: The automated calculation of costs, including the subtotal (quantity times price), tax, and the final total, ensuring consistency across all records.
1.4Business Value
–Accurate Reporting: By enforcing a strict structure, we ensure that financial reports regarding supplier spending and inventory costs are always based on complete and reliable data.
–Operational Efficiency: Standardizing how we record purchases reduces manual errors and makes it easier for different parts of the ERP system to communicate and share information.
–Audit Readiness: Having a consistent, validated history of all purchases makes it significantly easier to track spending patterns and verify transactions during internal or external audits.
