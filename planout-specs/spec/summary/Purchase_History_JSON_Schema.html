1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Purchase History feature establishes a standardized digital format for recording every product purchase made by the organization. This structure ensures that all purchase data—including product details, supplier information, and financial breakdowns—is captured consistently across the system. Additionally, the feature includes an automated verification step to ensure that all financial calculations (such as subtotals and final totals) are mathematically accurate before the data is saved.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Data Entry: A purchase record is initiated, capturing essential details such as the unique purchase ID, product ID, supplier ID, delivery ID, and the date of the transaction.
2.Financial Input: The system records the quantity purchased, the unit price, and any applicable tax.
3.Automated Calculation: The system automatically calculates the subtotal (quantity multiplied by unit price) and the final total (subtotal plus tax).
4.Validation: The system performs a background check to compare the provided subtotal and total against the calculated values.
5.Confirmation: If the figures match, the record is validated and accepted; if there is a discrepancy, the record is flagged to prevent financial errors.
1.3Key Concepts
–Schema: A standardized "blueprint" that defines exactly what information must be included in a purchase record and how it should be formatted.
–Validation: An automated quality-control process that ensures the numbers provided in a record are mathematically consistent.
–Subtotal: The cost of the items purchased before any taxes are applied.
–Total: The final amount due, representing the sum of the subtotal and the tax.
1.4Business Value
–Data Integrity: By enforcing a strict format, we eliminate "dirty data" and ensure that every purchase record contains all necessary information for reporting and auditing.
–Error Prevention: The automated validation logic acts as a safeguard, preventing human or system entry errors from resulting in incorrect financial totals.
–Operational Efficiency: Standardizing this data allows different parts of the ERP system to communicate seamlessly, ensuring that inventory, supplier management, and accounting teams are all working from the same accurate information.
