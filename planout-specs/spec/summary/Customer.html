1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Customer feature for the Mini ERP project provides a comprehensive system for managing customer profiles, their associated vehicles, financial records, and membership programs. This structure ensures that all customer-related data—from basic contact information and billing details to specialized pricing and deposit history—is organized, linked, and easily searchable within the system.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Claims & Memberships: The system tracks post-sale claims and manages membership programs, allowing customers to enroll in specific loyalty or service tiers.
2.Financial Ledger: Deposits and deposit transactions are recorded to maintain an accurate balance for each customer, ensuring financial transparency.
3.Vehicle Registry: The system maintains a detailed registry of customer vehicles, categorized by make and model, to support service and maintenance tracking.
4.Pricing & Billing: Custom product pricing can be set for individual customers, and specific billing profiles can be stored to streamline invoicing.
5.Customer Profile Management: The core customer record acts as the central hub, linking to the store, sales team, and customer groups.
6.Data Integrity: The system enforces strict relationships between these records (e.g., ensuring a customer is assigned to a valid store) to prevent data errors and maintain consistency across the ERP.
1.3Key Concepts
–Customer Profile: The central record containing contact details, tax information, and account settings.
–Vehicle Registry: A database of customer-owned vehicles, including technical details like VIN, engine capacity, and service history.
–Deposit Ledger: A tracking system for customer funds held on account, which automatically updates balances based on transaction history.
–Membership Programs: Defined loyalty or service tiers that customers can join, which track points and expiration dates.
–Customer Groups: A way to categorize customers for reporting or organizational purposes.
–Referential Integrity: A safety mechanism that ensures data remains linked correctly (e.g., you cannot delete a store if it still has active customers assigned to it).
1.4Business Value
–Operational Efficiency: By centralizing customer, vehicle, and financial data, staff can access a 360-degree view of a customer in one place, reducing time spent searching across different systems.
–Financial Accuracy: Automated tracking of deposits and account balances minimizes manual entry errors and provides real-time financial insights.
–Improved Customer Service: Detailed vehicle and membership records allow for personalized service, such as targeted maintenance reminders or loyalty-based pricing.
–Scalability: The structured database design allows the business to grow its customer base and product offerings without losing control over data relationships or reporting accuracy.
