1.  Logic
1.1Overview

This feature provides a way to view exactly how much of a specific product is stored across your various warehouses. Instead of just showing a simple count, it retrieves detailed stock information, including items currently reserved for orders, items currently in transit, and the total financial value of the stock held at each location.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Identify the Product: You provide the unique ID of the product you want to check.
2.Verify Existence: The system confirms that the product exists in the database. If it cannot be found, the system returns an error.
3.Apply Filters: You can narrow down your search by providing specific warehouse details, such as a warehouse ID, name, or location code. You can also choose how many results to see at once using pagination.
4.Retrieve and Calculate: The system gathers the stock data for that product across all relevant warehouses. It then performs real-time calculations to determine the reserved stock, in-transit stock, and the total cost of the inventory on hand.
5.Return Results: The system sends back a summary of the total stock count and a detailed list of the records found, formatted for easy use.
1.3Key Concepts
–Reserved Quantity: Stock that is currently held for existing customer orders and is no longer available for new sales.
–In-Transit Quantity: Stock that is currently being moved between locations or is on its way from a supplier.
–Total Cost: The calculated financial value of the stock currently sitting in a specific warehouse.
–Pagination: A method to break large lists of data into smaller, manageable pages (using "page" and "limit" settings) to improve performance and readability.
–Filtering: The ability to search for specific warehouse data by using criteria like the warehouse name or location code.
1.4Business Value

This feature gives inventory managers and sales teams real-time visibility into stock availability. By distinguishing between "on-hand," "reserved," and "in-transit" quantities, the business can make more accurate promises to customers regarding delivery dates and avoid overselling products. Additionally, having the total cost per warehouse readily available helps with financial reporting and inventory valuation.

2.  Data
2.1Overview

This feature provides a simple way to check the current inventory levels for a specific product across all company warehouses. By requesting information for a particular item, the system will return a clear list showing exactly how many units are available at each warehouse location, along with the last time that stock count was updated.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Product Verification: The system first checks to ensure the product you are inquiring about actually exists in the database. If the product cannot be found, the system will notify you that the request is invalid.
2.Data Retrieval: Once the product is confirmed, the system searches the inventory records to find all entries associated with that specific item.
3.Information Consolidation: The system gathers the relevant details, matching the stock numbers with the specific names of the warehouses where they are stored.
4.Final Output: The system presents a list containing the warehouse name, the current quantity on hand, and the timestamp of the most recent inventory update.
2.3Key Concepts
–Product: The specific item you are tracking within the Lumi ERP system.
–Warehouse Stock: The record of how many units of a product are physically located at a specific warehouse.
–Quantity on Hand: The current count of available items ready for use or sale.
–Last Updated: A timestamp indicating when the stock level for that specific warehouse was most recently recorded or modified.
2.4Business Value

This feature improves operational transparency by allowing staff to quickly identify where stock is located without needing to manually check individual warehouse logs. By providing real-time visibility into inventory distribution, the business can make faster decisions regarding order fulfillment, stock transfers between locations, and replenishment needs, ultimately reducing the risk of overselling or stockouts.
