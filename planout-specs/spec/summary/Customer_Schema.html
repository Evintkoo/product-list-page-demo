1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Customer Schema feature provides a structured way to manage customer information within the Lumi ERP system. It allows the business to categorize customers into specific types (Retail, Wholesale, Franchise, or Consignment), maintain detailed contact and tax information, and track their purchase history. This feature ensures that customer data is organized, searchable, and easily updatable, supporting better operational oversight.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Customer Creation: When a new customer is added, the system captures essential details (such as name, store, and company) and assigns a "type." The system automatically sets the customer as "active" and records the creation timestamp and the user who performed the action.
2.Categorization: Based on the selected type, the system applies specific internal flags (Wholesale, Franchise, or Consignment) to ensure the customer is correctly classified for reporting and business logic.
3.Data Management & Retrieval: Users can view a list of customers. This list is dynamic, allowing for filtering by criteria such as name, region, phone number, or status. The system also supports sorting to help users find specific records quickly.
4.Status Updates: If a customer’s status changes (e.g., deactivating a customer), the system updates the record and logs the change, including the timestamp and the user responsible for the update.
5.Purchase History: The system allows users to view the purchase history for any specific customer, providing visibility into their transaction status, amounts, and associated documents.
1.3Key Concepts
–Customer Type: A classification system that distinguishes between Retail, Wholesale, Franchise, and Consignment customers to apply the correct business rules.
–Active Status: A toggle that determines if a customer is currently enabled in the system; this can be updated at any time.
–Filtering & Sorting: Tools that allow users to narrow down large lists of customers or purchase records based on specific fields like name, code, or date.
–Audit Tracking: The system automatically records who created or updated a customer record and when, ensuring data accountability.
–Purchase History: A dedicated view for each customer that lists their past transactions, including status (e.g., Draft or Completed) and financial amounts.
1.4Business Value
–Operational Efficiency: By centralizing customer data and providing robust search and filter capabilities, staff can quickly access the information they need to serve customers.
–Data Accuracy: Standardized schemas ensure that all customer records contain consistent information, reducing errors in reporting and communication.
–Improved Decision Making: The ability to categorize customers and view their purchase history allows the business to better understand customer behavior and tailor sales strategies accordingly.
–Accountability: Automated tracking of record creation and status changes provides a clear audit trail, which is essential for maintaining data integrity in an ERP environment.
