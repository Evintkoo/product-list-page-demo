1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The Deactivate Customer Modal is a safety feature designed to prevent accidental changes to your customer records. When you choose to deactivate a customer, this pop-up window appears to ask for a final confirmation, ensuring that you intentionally want to change the customer's status to "inactive" before the system processes the request.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initiation: The user triggers the deactivation process for a specific customer.
2.Confirmation Prompt: A modal window appears displaying the name of the customer and asking for confirmation to proceed.
3.User Decision:

* To Proceed: The user clicks the "OK" button. The system then updates the customer's status to inactive and closes the window.

* To Cancel: The user clicks the "Cancel" button or the "X" icon in the corner. The window closes immediately, and no changes are made to the customer's status.

4.Processing State: While the system is updating the customer's status, the "OK" button is temporarily disabled to prevent duplicate requests.
1.3Key Concepts
–Deactivation: A status change that marks a customer as inactive within the system.
–Confirmation Modal: A secondary window that requires user interaction to verify an action before it is finalized.
–Safety Lock: A feature that disables the "OK" button during processing to ensure the system remains stable and prevents accidental double-clicks.
–Graceful Exit: The ability to close the window at any time without affecting the customer's data.
1.4Business Value

This feature reduces the risk of human error by providing a "safety net" for sensitive administrative actions. By requiring a deliberate confirmation, it ensures that customer records are only deactivated when intended, maintaining the integrity of your customer database and preventing unnecessary disruptions to business operations.

2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The Deactivate Customer Modal is a safety feature designed to prevent accidental changes to customer records. When you choose to deactivate a customer, this modal appears to confirm your intent. It ensures that the system only updates the customer's status after you have explicitly verified the action, while also providing feedback if the system encounters any issues during the process.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Initiation: You select a customer and trigger the deactivation process, which opens the confirmation modal.
2.Confirmation: The modal displays the name of the customer you are about to deactivate and asks for your final confirmation.
3.Processing: Once you click "OK," the system begins the deactivation request. During this time, the "OK" button is disabled to prevent you from accidentally sending the request multiple times.
4.Success: If the request is successful, the modal closes automatically, and the customer list refreshes to show the updated status.
5.Error Handling: If the system cannot complete the request (e.g., due to a connection issue), the modal remains open, and an error message is displayed so you know exactly what went wrong.
2.3Key Concepts
–Deactivation: A status change that marks a customer as inactive in the system without deleting their record.
–Submission Protection: A safeguard that disables the "OK" button while the system is working, ensuring that only one request is processed at a time.
–Error Feedback: A clear notification displayed within the modal if the system fails to update the customer status, allowing you to address the issue before trying again.
–List Refresh: An automatic update of your customer dashboard that ensures you are always viewing the most current information after a change is made.
2.4Business Value

This feature provides a controlled and reliable way to manage customer statuses. By requiring a confirmation step, it reduces the risk of human error. Additionally, the built-in error handling and submission protection ensure that the system remains stable and that users are always informed about the outcome of their actions, leading to more accurate data management within the ERP.

3.  Data
3.1Overview

The Deactivate Customer Modal is a safety feature designed to prevent accidental changes to your customer records. When you choose to deactivate a customer, this modal appears to confirm your intent, ensuring that you are modifying the correct account before the system processes the change.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Selection: You select a specific customer from your list to deactivate.
2.Data Retrieval: The system identifies the selected customer’s unique ID, their name, and their current status to ensure the request is accurate.
3.Confirmation: A modal window displays the customer's name, asking you to confirm that you wish to proceed with the deactivation.
4.Processing: Once you confirm, the system sends a request to update that specific customer's status to "inactive."
3.3Key Concepts
–Customer Selection: The specific record identified by the system to ensure the correct account is targeted for deactivation.
–Confirmation Modal: A pop-up window that acts as a final check to prevent errors or accidental clicks.
–Status Update: The final action taken by the system to change a customer's account from "active" to "inactive."
3.4Business Value

This feature reduces the risk of human error by providing a clear "stop-and-think" moment before permanent changes are made to customer records. By verifying the customer's identity and status before processing, the system maintains data integrity and prevents the accidental deactivation of active business accounts.
