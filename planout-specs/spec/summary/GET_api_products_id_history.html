1.  LOgic
1.1Overview

This feature provides a way to view the historical purchase prices for a specific product. By retrieving this data, you can see how much you have paid for an item over time, broken down by the different vendors you have worked with. This helps you monitor price fluctuations and evaluate vendor performance.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Request Initiation: You provide the unique ID of the product you wish to investigate. You can also specify how many records to see at once (limit) and which page of results to view.
2.Validation: The system checks that the product ID is formatted correctly. If the ID is invalid, the system will notify you of the error.
3.Verification: The system confirms that the product actually exists in your database. If the product cannot be found, the system will return a "Not Found" message.
4.Data Retrieval: The system gathers the purchase history records for that specific product, organizing them from the most recent date to the oldest.
5.Display: The system presents the list of historical prices, including the vendor involved and the date each price was effective.
1.3Key Concepts
–Purchase Price History: A chronological record of what was paid for a specific item, allowing for trend analysis.
–Pagination: A method of breaking large lists of data into smaller, manageable chunks (pages) to ensure the system remains fast and easy to read.
–Vendor Association: Each price record is linked to a specific vendor, allowing you to compare costs across different suppliers.
–Effective Date: The specific date on which a particular purchase price became active.
1.4Business Value
–Cost Transparency: Easily track price trends over time to identify when costs are rising or falling.
–Vendor Accountability: Compare historical pricing across different suppliers to ensure you are getting the best value for your purchases.
–Informed Decision Making: Use historical data to negotiate better contracts or plan future procurement budgets with greater accuracy.
2.  DATA
2.1Overview

The Product Price History feature allows users to view a chronological record of purchase prices for a specific product. By retrieving this data, you can see how much you have paid for an item over time and compare pricing across different vendors. The system provides this information in a paginated list, ensuring that even products with long histories are easy to navigate and review.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Request Initiation: The system receives a request for the price history of a specific product, including optional settings for how many records to show per page.
2.Validation: The system checks if the requested product exists in the database. If the product ID is invalid, the request is rejected with a "Not Found" error.
3.Parameter Check: The system verifies that the pagination settings (page number and limit) are valid numbers. If they are incorrect, the request is rejected.
4.Data Retrieval: The system fetches the price history records for that product, linking each price to the specific vendor who provided it.
5.Sorting: The records are organized so that the most recent price changes appear at the top of the list.
6.Response: The system returns the requested list of prices along with metadata (such as the total number of records) to help manage the display of the information.
2.3Key Concepts
–Pagination: A method of breaking a large list of data into smaller, manageable chunks (pages) to improve performance and readability.
–Vendor Association: Linking each price entry to a specific supplier, allowing you to track which vendor offered a particular price at a specific time.
–Effective Date: The specific date and time when a price change was recorded, which serves as the basis for the chronological history.
–Metadata: Supporting information returned with the data, such as the total count of records, which helps the user interface know how many pages of history are available.
2.4Business Value

This feature provides transparency into procurement costs, enabling better decision-making. By analyzing price trends over time, your team can identify cost fluctuations, evaluate vendor performance, and negotiate better contracts based on historical data. It transforms raw purchase records into actionable insights, helping you maintain tighter control over your supply chain expenses.
