1.  Data
1.1Overview

The "Stock Outgoing" feature allows the system to track inventory items leaving a store or warehouse for reasons other than a direct sale. This process ensures that stock levels are accurately reduced and that the financial impact is recorded in the company's accounting journals. The feature manages the entire lifecycle of these movements, from initial draft creation to final approval.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Creation: A user creates a "Stock Outgoing" document in a "Draft" state. The system validates that the requested items are available at the selected source location.
2.Drafting & Editing: Users can update, add, or remove items from the draft as needed.
3.Confirmation: Once the details are finalized, the document is moved to a "Confirmed" state. The system performs a final check to ensure the stock is still available.
4.Approval: Upon approval, the system performs several critical actions:

* It locks the document to prevent further changes.

* It calculates the final costs for the items.

* It permanently reduces the inventory levels at the source.

* It generates a formal journal entry to reflect the financial movement.

* The document status is updated to "Approved."

5.Listing & Viewing: Users can search, filter, and view a list of all stock outgoing records, or drill down into the details of a specific document to see its history, linked work orders, and financial impact.
1.3Key Concepts
–Stock Outgoing: A record representing inventory leaving a specific location (store or warehouse) without an associated customer sale.
–Workflow State: The status of a document, which dictates what actions can be taken (e.g., "Draft" can be edited, "Approved" is final).
–Source: The specific location (store or warehouse) from which the inventory is being removed.
–Journal Entry: An accounting record created automatically upon approval to ensure the inventory reduction is reflected in the company's financial books.
–Line Items: The individual products included in a single stock outgoing document, each with its own quantity and cost details.
1.4Business Value
–Inventory Accuracy: By requiring a formal approval process and real-time stock checks, the system prevents inventory discrepancies and ensures that stock levels always reflect reality.
–Financial Integrity: The automatic generation of journal entries ensures that every movement of goods is accounted for financially, reducing manual bookkeeping errors.
–Operational Visibility: The ability to list, filter, and track these movements provides management with clear insights into where inventory is going and why, supporting better decision-making and auditing.
