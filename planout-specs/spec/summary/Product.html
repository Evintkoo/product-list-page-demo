1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Product feature serves as the central hub for managing inventory and catalog information within the Mini ERP system. It is designed to track not only the core details of a product (such as its name, brand, and type) but also its movement, pricing history, and availability across different physical locations like stores and warehouses.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Classification: Products are organized by specific types and brands to ensure consistent categorization.
2.Store and Warehouse Setup: The system defines the physical locations (Stores and Warehouses) where stock can be held.
3.Inventory Tracking: The system monitors stock levels at these locations, including reserved or consigned items, and maintains a detailed history of stock changes (increases or decreases).
4.Pricing Management: The system tracks various price points for products, allowing for different pricing strategies based on the region, store, or customer group.
5.Product Lifecycle: Every product is assigned a unique identifier (SKU) and can be tagged for easier searching. The system maintains a comprehensive history of changes to product details and purchase records to ensure full auditability.
1.3Key Concepts
–Product (SKU): The primary record for an item, containing its core identity, pricing, and status (e.g., active, frozen, or stock-tracked).
–Stock Management: The ability to view inventory levels across different physical locations, distinguishing between available, reserved, and consigned stock.
–Pricing Tracking: A flexible mechanism to record how prices change over time and vary depending on the sales channel or location.
–Audit History: Dedicated logs that capture changes to product information and purchase history, providing a clear trail of who updated what and when.
–Categorization: The use of Brands, Types, and Tags to group products logically, making it easier to manage large catalogs.
1.4Business Value
–Operational Efficiency: By centralizing product data and stock levels, staff can quickly identify where items are located and how much is available, reducing stockouts or overstocking.
–Financial Accuracy: Detailed purchase and price tracking ensures that the business can accurately calculate costs, margins, and tax obligations.
–Scalability: The structured approach to brands, types, and tags allows the business to grow its product catalog without losing control over organization.
–Accountability: With built-in history tracking for both product changes and stock movements, the business gains full visibility into inventory discrepancies and pricing adjustments.
