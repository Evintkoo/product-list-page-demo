1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Product feature serves as the central hub for managing inventory and catalog information within the ERP system. It provides a structured way to define products, categorize them by brand and type, and track their movement, pricing, and history across different locations (stores and warehouses). This system ensures that every item has a unique identifier and that all changes—whether in stock levels or pricing—are recorded for audit and operational accuracy.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Product Definition: New products are created with unique identifiers (SKUs), names, and classifications (Brand and Type).
2.Inventory Tracking: The system monitors stock levels separately for retail stores and central warehouses, ensuring accurate availability counts.
3.Stock Movement: Any change in stock (increases or decreases) at a store is logged in a history table, capturing the reason for the change (e.g., sales or transfers).
4.Pricing Management: The system maintains a history of price changes and allows for specialized pricing (such as regional or customer-group specific rates) to be tracked against the product.
5.Operational Metadata: Products can be tagged for easier organization, and the system maintains a comprehensive purchase history to track how and when items were acquired.
1.3Key Concepts
–SKU (Stock Keeping Unit): A unique code assigned to each product to ensure it can be identified and tracked accurately across the entire system.
–Foreign Key: A logical link that connects data across tables; for example, ensuring that a "Store Stock" record is always tied to a valid "Product."
–Stock Tracking: The ability to monitor inventory levels in real-time across different locations, including reserved or consignment stock in warehouses.
–Audit History: Dedicated tables that record changes to product status, pricing, and stock levels, providing a clear trail of who changed what and when.
–Price Tracking: A flexible mechanism to manage different price tiers (e.g., wholesale vs. retail) and apply specific price overrides based on the location or customer group.
1.4Business Value
–Operational Efficiency: By centralizing product data and automating stock updates, the system reduces manual entry errors and provides a single source of truth for inventory.
–Informed Decision Making: With detailed purchase and price history, management can analyze trends, track supplier performance, and make better-informed pricing strategies.
–Scalability: The modular design allows the business to easily add new stores, warehouses, or product categories without disrupting existing operations.
–Accountability: The audit logs for stock changes and price updates ensure transparency and security, making it easier to reconcile inventory and investigate discrepancies.
