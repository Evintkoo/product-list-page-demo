1.  Data
1.1Overview

This feature defines how product information is organized and stored within the ERP system. It sets up the "Product" as the central piece of information that everything else in the system connects to — including what brand and type a product belongs to, how it's priced in different locations, how its stock is tracked across stores and warehouses, and a complete history of changes made to it over time. In short, this is the foundation that lets the business reliably track every product it sells, from creation through pricing changes to stock movements.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Set up lookup lists first: Before a product can be created, the system needs two reference lists — Product Brands (e.g., Toyota, Honda) and Product Types (e.g., Spare Part, Accessory). These act like dropdown menus that keep naming consistent across all products.
2.Create the core Product record: Each product is registered with essential details — a unique SKU code, product name, which brand and type it belongs to, whether it's currently active for sale, whether stores can override its price, its tax category, and whether its stock is automatically tracked.
3.Track every price and status change: Whenever a product's pricing (supply price, wholesale price, retail price, trade-in value, warranty) or its active/tracked/bundle status changes, the system automatically logs a snapshot of that change, including who made it and when — creating a permanent, unchangeable history trail.
4.Apply location- or group-specific pricing: Products can have different override prices depending on where they're sold or who's buying — by region, specific store, specific warehouse, or customer group — allowing flexible pricing strategies without altering the base product record.
5.Tag products for easy grouping: Products can be labeled with tags (e.g., "On Sale," "New Arrival") to help with searching, filtering, and marketing efforts.
6.Record every purchase transaction: Every time stock is purchased from a supplier, a permanent record is kept — including quantity, price, tax, and total cost — creating a full purchasing history per product.
7.Track stock at the warehouse level: Each warehouse keeps a running count of how much of a product it has in inventory, how much is reserved for pending orders, how much is on consignment, and how much is actually available to sell.
8.Track stock at the store level: Similarly, each store keeps its own running stock count for every product.
9.Log every store stock change: Any time stock at a store increases or decreases (due to a sale, stock transfer, or other event), the system records the event details — what changed, by how much, and why — before updating the store's current stock total.
1.3Key Concepts
–Product: The central record representing an item the business sells, holding its identity, pricing rules, and status flags.
–SKU: A unique code used to identify and look up a product across all parts of the system — like a product's fingerprint.
–Brand & Type: Standardized categories that describe what a product is and who makes it, kept in separate reference lists to avoid inconsistent naming.
–Price Tracking: A way to set different prices for the same product depending on the region, store, warehouse, or customer group it's sold to.
–Product History: A permanent audit trail showing how a product's price and status have changed over time, along with who made each change.
–Purchase History: A permanent log of every time stock of a product was bought from a supplier, including cost and tax details.
–Warehouse Stock / Store Stock: Running totals showing how much of a product is available at each warehouse or store.
–Store Stock History: A permanent log of every stock adjustment at a store, showing what caused the change (a sale, transfer, etc.).
–Product Tags: Labels attached to products to support grouping, searching, and marketing.
–Frozen Product: A status that prevents a product from being moved between branches, typically used to control inventory movement temporarily.
1.4Business Value

This structure gives the business a single, reliable source of truth for all product-related information, reducing errors caused by duplicate or inconsistent data. Automatic history tracking for pricing and stock changes ensures full accountability and makes it easy to answer "who changed what, and when" — valuable for audits, dispute resolution, and financial reporting. Flexible pricing by region, store, or customer group enables smarter sales strategies without complicating the core product setup. Meanwhile, real-time stock visibility across both warehouses and stores helps prevent overselling, supports better purchasing decisions, and improves overall inventory accuracy — all of which contribute to smoother operations and better customer service.
