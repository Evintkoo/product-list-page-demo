1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The "Stock Outgoing Details" feature provides a centralized view for managing the lifecycle of a stock outgoing document. It allows authorized users to track, review, and process the movement of inventory from a specific location. The interface is designed to be state-aware, meaning the options available to the user change automatically based on whether the document is a draft, confirmed, approved, or cancelled.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Viewing the Document: When you open a stock outgoing document, the system displays the full header information (such as reference codes, dates, and source locations) and a table of all products involved.
2.Workflow Management: Depending on the current status of the document, you will see different action buttons:

* Draft: You can print, confirm the document, or cancel it. You can also edit the details.

* Confirmed: You can print, approve, or cancel the document. Editing is disabled to ensure data integrity.

* Approved: The document is finalized. You can only print it. Additional accounting information (such as the reversal account and journal reference) becomes visible.

* Cancelled: You can print the document or reset it back to a draft state if further changes are needed.

3.Approval Process: If you choose to approve a confirmed document, you are taken to a dedicated approval screen. Here, you can review the final costs (which are recalculated to ensure accuracy) and assign a "reversal account" for each item. Once saved, the document is officially approved and the accounting journal is generated.
1.3Key Concepts
–Workflow State: The status of the document (Draft, Confirmed, Approved, or Cancelled) which dictates what actions you can take and what information is visible.
–Reversal Account: An accounting category assigned during the approval process that determines how the stock movement is recorded in the financial system.
–Stock Check: A validation process that occurs when you move a document from "Draft" to "Confirmed" to ensure the requested quantity is actually available in the warehouse.
–Recalculated Cost: The system automatically updates the average cost of items at the moment of approval to ensure the financial records reflect the most current inventory valuation.
1.4Business Value

This feature ensures accurate inventory control and financial reporting by enforcing a strict approval workflow. By separating the "Draft" and "Approval" stages, the system prevents unauthorized changes to confirmed documents and ensures that accounting data—specifically the cost of goods and reversal accounts—is accurate and up-to-date at the exact moment the stock leaves the facility. This reduces manual errors and provides a clear audit trail for all inventory movements.

2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The "Stock Outgoing Details" feature provides a comprehensive view of a single inventory outgoing document. It allows users to see all relevant information—such as the source location, product details, and current status—in one place. The system also includes a workflow management tool that allows users to transition the document through various stages (such as confirming, canceling, or resetting) directly from the view page.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Loading the Document: When you open a stock outgoing record, the system automatically retrieves all header information (like dates, creator, and reference codes) and the full list of items in a single request.
2.Displaying Information: The page populates two main areas:

* Header Details: Displays administrative information, including the source (Warehouse or Store), approval dates, and the current workflow status.

* Item Table: Lists every product included in the outgoing stock, showing quantities, costs, and account details.

3.Conditional Visibility: Certain fields, such as the "Journal" reference or "Reversal Account," are hidden until the document reaches the "Approved" status to ensure users only see relevant data for the current stage.
4.Workflow Actions: Depending on the document's current status, the system displays action buttons (e.g., Confirm, Cancel, or Reset). Clicking these buttons immediately updates the document's status.
5.Automatic Refresh: Once an action is performed, the page automatically updates the workflow bar, dates, and visibility of specific fields based on the new status, ensuring the information remains accurate without needing to manually refresh the page.
2.3Key Concepts
–Workflow State: The current stage of the document (e.g., Draft, Confirmed, Approved, or Cancelled), which determines which actions you can take and what information is visible.
–Header Mapping: The process of organizing administrative data (like dates and reference codes) into a clear, readable summary at the top of the page.
–Item Table: A detailed list of all products associated with the outgoing stock, including financial data like average costs and account codes.
–Workflow Actions: Direct commands (Confirm, Cancel, Reset) that change the document's status and trigger immediate updates to the page display.
2.4Business Value

This feature centralizes inventory management by providing a "single source of truth" for outgoing stock. By displaying all necessary details and workflow controls on one screen, it reduces the need for users to navigate between multiple pages. The automatic status updates and conditional visibility ensure that users always have the most accurate, stage-appropriate information, which minimizes errors and streamlines the inventory approval process.

3.  Data
3.1Overview

The "Stock Outgoing Details" feature provides a comprehensive view of a single inventory outgoing transaction. It allows users to see all relevant information—such as the source warehouse, items being moved, and the current status of the transaction—in one centralized location. The feature also manages the lifecycle of the transaction, guiding it from a draft state through to final approval and accounting integration.

3.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.View Details: When you open a stock outgoing record, the system retrieves the full document, including the header information (such as dates, references, and status) and the list of all items involved.
2.Workflow Management: Depending on the current status of the document, you can perform specific actions:

* Draft: You can confirm the document (which checks if the items are available in stock), cancel it, or edit the details.

* Confirmed: You can proceed to the approval screen or cancel the document.

* Approved: The document is finalized; no further workflow changes are allowed, but you can print the record.

* Cancelled: You can reset the document back to a draft state if needed.

3.Approval Process: When you select "Approve," you are taken to a dedicated screen to finalize the transaction. Saving this screen updates the costs, assigns reversal accounts, generates the necessary accounting journal entry, and marks the document as officially approved.
3.3Key Concepts
–Workflow State: The current stage of the document (e.g., Draft, Confirmed, Approved, or Cancelled), which determines which actions you are permitted to take.
–Stock Availability Check: A validation step that occurs during confirmation to ensure that the requested items are actually available in the warehouse before moving forward.
–Journal Entry: An automated accounting record created upon final approval that reflects the financial impact of the stock movement.
–Reversal Account: A specific accounting account used to balance the transaction, which becomes visible only after the document has been officially approved.
3.4Business Value
–Operational Transparency: Provides a single source of truth for stock movements, ensuring that warehouse staff and managers can see exactly what is being moved and why.
–Error Prevention: By validating stock availability before confirmation, the system prevents the creation of invalid or impossible outgoing transactions.
–Accounting Accuracy: Automating the generation of journal entries and the assignment of reversal accounts upon approval reduces manual data entry errors and ensures that inventory costs are accurately reflected in the company's financial records.
