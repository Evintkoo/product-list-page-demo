1.  Frontend
1.1Overview

The "Customer Purchases" page is a read-only interface within the Lumi ERP system that allows staff to view a complete history of retail purchases made by a specific customer. This page is integrated into the customer profile section, ensuring that sales data is easily accessible while navigating through customer details.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Security Verification: When the page loads, the system automatically checks if the user is currently logged in. If the session is invalid or expired, the user is redirected to the login page.
2.Data Retrieval: Once authenticated, the system fetches the purchase history specifically for the selected customer using their unique identifier.
3.Loading State: While the system retrieves the data from the database, a "Loading..." indicator is displayed to the user.
4.Data Presentation: Once the data is retrieved, the system populates a table with the customer's purchase records, including details such as the document number, transaction status, total amount, store location, and the creator of the record.
5.Navigation: Users can continue to use the main navigation menus or switch between different customer detail tabs (such as profile or other history) without leaving the customer context.
1.3Key Concepts
–Authentication Check: A security step that ensures only authorized users can view sensitive customer financial data.
–Read-Only Interface: A design approach where the user can view and analyze information but cannot modify or delete the purchase records directly from this screen.
–Customer Context: The system maintains the specific customer's identity throughout the page, ensuring that the displayed purchase list is always relevant to the individual being viewed.
–Data Mapping: The process of taking raw information from the system's database and organizing it into clear, labeled columns (Document Number, Status, Amount, Store, and Created By) for the user.
1.4Business Value

This feature provides immediate visibility into a customer's purchasing behavior, which is essential for customer support, sales analysis, and account management. By centralizing this information within the customer profile, staff can quickly answer inquiries about past transactions, verify order statuses, and understand a customer's history without needing to search through separate sales reports or accounting logs.

2.  Logic
2.1Overview

The "Purchased Retails List" feature provides a clear, organized view of a specific customer's shopping history directly within their profile page. This feature ensures that staff can quickly review past transactions, including order status and financial details, to better assist customers and manage account history.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Security Check: When the page loads, the system automatically verifies that you are currently logged in. If your session has expired or you are not authenticated, you will be redirected to the login screen.
2.Data Retrieval: Once authenticated, the system fetches the complete list of purchase records associated with the specific customer you are viewing.
3.Information Formatting: The system organizes the raw purchase data into a user-friendly table, ensuring that key details like document numbers, transaction status, and purchase amounts are clearly displayed.
4.Display: The final list is presented on the Customer Detail page, allowing for immediate review of the customer's retail activity.
2.3Key Concepts
–Authentication: A security step that confirms the user has permission to view sensitive customer data before the page displays any information.
–Purchase Record: A digital summary of a customer's transaction, containing details such as the document ID, the current status of the order, the total amount spent, the store location, and the customer identifier.
–Data Mapping: The process of taking raw information from our database and translating it into readable labels (e.g., "Document Number," "Status," "Amount") so that the information is easy for staff to interpret at a glance.
2.4Business Value

This feature improves operational efficiency by centralizing customer history. Instead of searching through multiple systems or physical files, staff can instantly access a customer's purchase behavior. This enables faster customer support, more accurate account management, and a better overall understanding of customer engagement with the business.
