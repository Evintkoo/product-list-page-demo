1.  Logic
1.1Overview

This feature provides a way for the system to retrieve a list of payment types that have been assigned to specific stores. It allows users to see which payment methods are currently active or inactive for their business locations. The information is automatically restricted to the user's own company, ensuring data security and relevance.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Authentication: The system identifies the user and automatically locks the request to their specific company.
2.Parameter Collection: The system gathers any optional filters provided by the user, such as a specific store, a specific payment type, or the active status of the mapping.
3.Pagination Setup: The system checks if the user requested a specific number of results (limit) or a starting point (offset). If not provided, it defaults to showing the first 20 results.
4.Data Retrieval: The system searches the database for all mappings that match the user's company and the selected filters.
5.Ordering: The results are organized by the date they were created, starting with the oldest entries.
6.Response: The system returns the requested list of mappings along with a total count of all available records to help with navigation.
1.3Key Concepts
–Payment Type Mapping: The link between a specific store and a payment method (e.g., credit card, cash, or digital wallet) that determines if that method is available for use at that location.
–Filtering: The ability to narrow down the list by selecting a specific store, a specific payment type, or by viewing only active/inactive mappings.
–Pagination: A method of breaking a large list of data into smaller, manageable pages to improve performance and readability.
–Scope: A security measure that ensures users can only view data belonging to their own company.
1.4Business Value

This feature enables store managers and administrators to easily audit and manage payment configurations across their organization. By providing a clear view of which payment methods are enabled at which locations, businesses can ensure their point-of-sale systems are correctly configured, troubleshoot payment issues, and maintain consistency across all store branches.

2.  Data
2.1Overview

The `GET /api/payment-type-mappings` feature provides a way to retrieve a list of payment methods that have been assigned to specific stores within your company. This tool allows you to see which payment types are currently active or inactive for each store, helping you manage your payment configurations efficiently. The system automatically ensures that you only see data relevant to your own company.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Authentication Check: The system identifies your company automatically based on your login credentials, ensuring you only access your own organization's data.
2.Applying Filters: You can narrow down the list by providing specific criteria, such as a particular store, a specific payment type, or the current status (active/inactive).
3.Counting Records: The system calculates the total number of records that match your filters to help with navigation.
4.Sorting and Pagination: The system organizes the results by the date they were created (oldest to newest) and breaks the list into manageable pages based on your requested limit and offset.
5.Data Delivery: The system returns the requested list of mappings along with metadata (such as the total count) to help your interface display the information correctly.
2.3Key Concepts
–Payment Type Mapping: A record that links a specific payment method (like Credit Card or Cash) to a specific store location.
–Filtering: The ability to search for specific records by store, payment type, or status without having to scroll through the entire list.
–Pagination: A method of displaying large amounts of data in smaller, easier-to-read chunks (pages) to improve performance and usability.
–Metadata: Additional information provided with your results, such as the total number of items found, which helps the application know how many pages of data exist.
2.4Business Value

This feature provides transparency into how payment methods are distributed across your retail locations. By allowing users to filter and view these mappings, the business can:

* Ensure Accuracy: Quickly verify that the correct payment methods are enabled for the right stores.

* Improve Efficiency: Easily identify and update the status of payment types across the organization.

* Maintain Control: Provide a secure, scoped view of configuration data that is restricted to the authenticated user's company, preventing unauthorized access to other organizations' settings.
