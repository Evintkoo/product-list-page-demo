1.  Data
1.1Overview

The Supplier feature provides a centralized database structure for managing business relationships with suppliers within the Lumi ERP system. It acts as the "source of truth" for supplier information, including contact details, financial terms, and specific operational settings. The system is designed to handle various types of suppliers—including standard, internal, and consignment-based partners—while maintaining a clear audit trail of changes and financial movements.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Supplier Registration: A new supplier is created with essential details such as name, contact information, and financial settings (like credit limits and payment terms).
2.Operational Categorization: The system flags the supplier based on their role (e.g., internal vs. external, or consignment-based) to ensure they appear in the correct filtered lists and are handled correctly by the system's purchasing logic.
3.Product Mapping: Specific products are linked to the supplier, allowing the system to track unique discount rates and pricing terms for each item provided by that supplier.
4.Financial Tracking: If the supplier requires deposits, the system tracks the balance and records every transaction (payments made or deposits consumed) in a dedicated ledger.
5.Audit & History: Every time a supplier's core information (like address or credit terms) is updated, the system automatically saves a snapshot of the previous details to a history log, ensuring full accountability.
1.3Key Concepts
–Supplier: The primary record representing a business entity that provides goods or services.
–Internal Supplier: A special category for intra-group suppliers that are managed within the same organization.
–Consignment: A business model where goods are held by the company but only paid for once they are sold; the system tracks these suppliers separately to manage "Consignment Closure."
–Supplier Deposit: A financial balance held with a supplier, used to manage prepayments or down payments.
–Product Mapping: A link between a supplier and a product that defines specific purchasing terms, such as discount rates.
–Audit History: A secure, read-only log that tracks changes to supplier details over time for compliance and reporting.
1.4Business Value
–Operational Efficiency: By centralizing supplier data, the system ensures that purchasing teams always have access to accurate contact, tax, and financial information, reducing manual errors.
–Financial Control: Automated tracking of credit limits, payment terms, and deposit balances helps the finance team manage cash flow and prevent over-purchasing.
–Regulatory Compliance: The built-in audit history provides a clear record of who changed supplier information and when, which is essential for internal audits and transparency.
–Flexible Purchasing: The ability to distinguish between standard and consignment suppliers allows the business to automate complex accounting processes, such as calculating payments only when consignment stock is sold.
