1.  Logic
1.1Overview

This feature provides a way to retrieve a detailed log of all stock changes within your stores. By using this tool, you can track inventory movements over time, see who performed specific updates, and filter records to find exactly what you need. The system returns this information in organized, manageable pages to ensure the data remains easy to navigate.

1.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Authentication: The system verifies that you are logged in and have an active session before allowing access to the data.
2.Filter Application: You can narrow down your search by applying specific criteria, such as selecting a particular store, choosing the type of transaction (e.g., restock or sale), setting a date range, or searching for the person who recorded the change.
3.Data Retrieval: The system processes your filters and fetches the relevant stock history records from the database.
4.Pagination: To keep the information organized, the system breaks the results into pages, providing you with the current page number, the number of items per page, and the total count of records found.
5.Response: The system delivers the requested list of records along with the pagination details. If an error occurs, the system provides a clear notification that the request could not be completed.
1.3Key Concepts
–Stock History: A chronological record of every change made to your inventory, providing an audit trail for your stock levels.
–Filtering: The ability to refine your search results by specific attributes like store location, transaction type, date, or the user who performed the action.
–Pagination: A method of splitting large amounts of data into smaller, readable chunks (pages) to improve performance and usability.
–Audit Trail: The capability to see "who, what, and when" regarding inventory changes, which is essential for accountability and troubleshooting.
1.4Business Value

This feature is critical for maintaining inventory accuracy and operational transparency. By providing a searchable history of stock movements, it allows managers to:

* Identify Discrepancies: Quickly investigate why stock levels might not match expectations by reviewing past transactions.

* Improve Accountability: Track which staff members are performing specific inventory updates.

* Analyze Trends: Use historical data to understand peak activity times or common transaction patterns, helping to optimize future stock management decisions.

2.  Data
2.1Overview

This feature provides a way to retrieve a detailed history of stock changes for a specific store. By using this tool, users can audit inventory adjustments, track when stock levels were modified, and identify who performed those changes. The system allows for flexible searching and filtering to help users quickly find the specific transaction records they need.

2.2Step-by-Step Flow
1.Request Submission: The user sends a request to view stock history, optionally including filters such as the specific store, the type of transaction, a date range, or the name of the person who made the change.
2.Filtering: The system processes these criteria to narrow down the search results to only the relevant records.
3.Data Retrieval: The system queries the database to collect all matching stock history entries.
4.Pagination: To ensure the system remains fast and easy to navigate, the results are returned in organized pages rather than one massive list.
5.Response: The system returns the requested list of records. If an unexpected issue occurs during this process, the system provides an error message to notify the user.
2.3Key Concepts
–Stock History: A chronological record of every time a store's inventory quantity was increased or decreased.
–Filtering: The ability to refine search results based on specific criteria, such as a date range or a specific staff member who recorded the change.
–Pagination: A method of breaking large amounts of data into smaller, manageable chunks (pages) to improve performance and readability.
–Transaction Type: A label used to categorize why a stock change occurred (e.g., a sale, a restock, or a return).
2.4Business Value

This feature enhances inventory transparency and accountability within the Mini ERP system. By maintaining a clear audit trail of stock movements, store managers can:

* Improve Accuracy: Quickly investigate discrepancies between recorded stock and physical inventory.

* Enhance Security: Monitor staff activity to ensure all stock adjustments are authorized and legitimate.

* Support Decision Making: Analyze historical trends in stock changes to better understand store performance and replenishment needs.
